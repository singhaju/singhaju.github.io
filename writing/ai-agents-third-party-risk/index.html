What I learned building AI agents for third-party risk

At Allianz Ayudhya I built two AI-agent prototypes for third-party risk that support people's judgement, and learned from the CISO how governance and risk management work in practice.

  • Risk management
  • AI agents
  • Internship

Every company depends on vendors: cloud services, software, consultants. Each vendor is a risk, so companies check them. This is third-party risk management (TPRM). From June to July 2026, I was an information security intern at Allianz Ayudhya in Bangkok, mentored by the Chief Information Security Officer, Apichai Wongchindakhun.

Two sides of the same problem

Allianz Ayudhya sits on both sides. As a supplier, the Information Security team fills in long due-diligence questionnaires by hand, searching internal documents for every answer. As a buyer, business owners who rarely do it must run the risk process for each new vendor. I spent my first weeks learning how the team did both: I followed the process from start to end on real, anonymized cases, read many documents, and learned from my mentor how to look at risk: what to check, what matters most, and why.

The gaps

Along the way, I learned that the process still has gaps: much of it is done by hand, and many steps depend on people making the right call. I wanted to help close those gaps, not by replacing people, but by supporting their judgement.

Agent 1: answering questionnaires

The first agent takes the first pass at a questionnaire. It reads the company's policy and evidence documents, matches each question to the evidence and drafts the answer. Every answer comes with:

  • the evidence it used, so a person can check it;
  • a reason, so the answer can be explained, including how to answer without giving away internal information;
  • a confidence score, so reviewers know where to look first.

A gap analysis shows where current controls fall short and suggests how to close each gap, and a fit-gap analysis compares the current controls with what the questionnaire asks for. But the agent only does the first job: a person checks every answer, and I designed it that way on purpose.

Agent 2: guiding business owners

The second agent is for business owners who are new to the process. It does not just help them fill in information: it teaches them to run the process themselves, guides them step by step, checks their inputs at each step and warns them about anything important they should not miss. It is a support tool, not one that does the work for them.

How I worked

Requirements with the team first, then the design, the build and tests on real, anonymized cases. I showed my progress often and changed both agents based on the team's feedback. I demonstrated the questionnaire agent on 6 July 2026 and presented both agents on 31 July 2026.

What I learned

The tools were the smaller part. From my mentor I learned how information security, governance and risk management work in practice, how each department works, and what a CISO's work involves.

Building the agents taught me my biggest lesson: AI is a tool, not everything. In security, it is at its best when it supports people, not when it replaces their thinking, and the responsibility for how we use it always stays with us. That is why every answer shows its evidence, its reason and how confident the agent is: a person can check the work instead of redoing it, and stays in charge of the final answer.
