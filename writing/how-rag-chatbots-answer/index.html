How a chatbot answers from your own documents

A plain explanation of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), the pipeline I built and tested as an R&D intern on the AI research team at Amity Solutions.

  • AI
  • LLM
  • Internship

A chatbot built on a large language model (LLM) only knows what it was trained on. Ask it about a company's own products or rules and it may guess, and a confident guess is worse than no answer.

Search first, then answer

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) fixes this with a simple order of steps:

  • The user asks a question.
  • The pipeline searches the knowledge base for the documents that match the question best.
  • It gives those documents and the question to the LLM.
  • The LLM writes the answer from those documents.

The model does not need retraining when the documents change. Update the knowledge base, and the next answer uses it.

What I did

In July and August 2023, I was an R&D intern on the AI research and development team at Amity Solutions in Bangkok. I built the pipeline behind the team's chatbots. I read recent research papers on LLMs, applied their ideas in the pipeline and tested them end to end, from the user's question to the final answer. I also did data analysis and prompt engineering for the team.

What I took from it

Test the whole path, not one part. A better model does not help if the search step brings back the wrong documents, so the question, the search and the answer have to be checked together.

It also changed how I look at AI security. If an LLM answers from whatever documents it is given, those documents are part of the input, and anyone who can change them can change the answer. The same idea comes back in my research on AI agents.
