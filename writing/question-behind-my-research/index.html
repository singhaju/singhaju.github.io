The question behind my research

Can a security check be cheap enough to run on every request, and still be provable afterwards? How that one question links my research projects.

  • Research
  • Access control
  • Cryptography

My research projects look different at first: Kubernetes access rules, zero-knowledge proofs, post-quantum signatures, AI agents, fog computing. Most of them come back to one question: can a security check be cheap enough to run on every request, and still be provable afterwards?

Why both matter

A check that is too slow gets skipped or turned off. A check that leaves no proof cannot be trusted later, by an auditor or after an incident. I want both.

The pattern I keep using

  • Do the heavy work once. Compile the rules ahead of time, or verify the caller carefully once and issue a short-lived pass.
  • Make each request cheap. Check a small token or a precompiled index, so each request does little work.
  • Keep a commitment for later. Merkle trees and ledger anchors make any change to rules or logs show.
  • Fail safe. A deny rule wins, and revoking access removes only what is affected.

Where each project fits

  • U-HAP (published, Best Paper at IEEE JCSSE 2026, 2nd of 4 authors): one model for every Kubernetes access rule, compiled ahead of time; deny always wins. Read the post
  • FEDAC (under review at IEEE TDSC, 1st of 4 authors): zero-trust checks across clusters with a short-lived pass. Read the post
  • ZKAudit (accepted at KST 2027): an auditor proves they may audit without saying who they are. Read the post
  • PQ-SCVA-MCK (submitted): post-quantum signed proofs for access decisions across clusters. Read the post
  • SWARM-Fog (submitted): edge servers that pull work like a swarm and recover from failures, with no central controller.
  • MoCA (in preparation): keeping an AI agent inside what its user allowed. Read the post
  • ZK-ALBA, an encrypted SIEM and FRESCO (in preparation): audit proofs bound to what each auditor may see, attack detection on encrypted logs shared by several companies, and freshness proofs for cloud services that scale up and down.

How I work on each one

Find the gap in published designs, design the scheme, build the prototype, measure it on AWS or real data, and write the paper. All of them are with my supervisor, Prof. Somchart Fugkeaw, at SIIT.

For work that is not published yet, I show the idea and what I built, not results. The full list is in the Research section.
